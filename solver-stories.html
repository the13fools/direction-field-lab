A vocabulary before the names

Ask four questions of every fluid method.

A method becomes much easier to compare when each design decision is placed on the right axis.

  1. 01
    What is stored?

    A grid velocity? Particles with velocity moments? Vorticity? A velocity one-form? Clebsch labels and depth?

  2. 02
    How is it transported?

    Backtrace from grid samples, move particles forward, transport labels, or update conservative cell fluxes.

  3. 03
    Where do forces act?

    On an Eulerian grid, on particles, through an energy, or through a surface differential operator.

  4. 04
    Which promise is enforced?

    Zero divergence, mass conservation, circulation, energy behavior, positivity of depth, or some deliberate compromise.

Three influential stories

They solve different parts of the problem.

Read left to right as a set of possible ingredients, not a tournament with one winner.

A
GRID TRANSPORT · STAM 1999

Backtrace the answer: Stable Fluids.

Stand at each grid sample at the new time, follow its characteristic backward, and interpolate the old field at the departure point. The same semi-Lagrangian idea can carry smoke density, velocity components, or Clebsch labels.

new value samples the old field at the backtraced point
WHY PEOPLE LOVE IT

Large steps remain visually stable and the grid makes forces and a pressure Poisson solve convenient.

WHAT IT SPENDS

Repeated interpolation smooths fine structure. “Stable” does not mean exact conservation, no numerical diffusion, or correct behavior at arbitrarily large steps.

Stam’s publication page ↗
B
PARTICLE–GRID TRANSFER · JIANG ET AL. 2015

Carry more local motion: APIC.

Particles move material through the domain while a background grid handles forces and constraints. APIC augments each particle’s translational velocity with a local affine velocity model, retaining information that ordinary PIC loses during particle–grid transfers.

each particle carries a locally affine velocity
WHY PEOPLE LOVE IT

It keeps PIC’s filtering while greatly reducing transfer dissipation, and preserves angular momentum across the transfer described in the method.

WHAT A SURFACE VERSION NEEDS

Tangent particle states, parallel transport between frames, particle–mesh kernels, boundary handling, and a decision about whether the physics is a thin layer or a free surface in ambient space.

APIC project + paper ↗
C
STATE REPRESENTATION · CLEBSCH + COVECTORS

Carry labels whose crossing remembers spin.

Clebsch variables factor the velocity one-form into scalar data. The labels can be transported semi-Lagrangianly, with particles, or by another characteristic method; afterward the one-form is reconstructed and coupled to the intended constraint or height equation.

exact, label, and harmonic pieces of the velocity one-form
WHY STUDY IT HERE

The representation makes material labels, vorticity, circulation, metric conversion, and topology visible in one pipeline.

WHAT IT DOES NOT CHOOSE FOR YOU

Transport accuracy, gauge, global charts, harmonic periods, pressure or height evolution, boundaries, and the final discretization all remain modeling decisions.

Other doors worth opening

The landscape is wider than these three.

These methods become relevant when the physical goal changes.

CONSERVATIVE FINITE VOLUMEWhen waves, shocks, and wet/dry fronts matter.

Numerical fluxes make mass conservation and discontinuous solutions central. This is often the more natural baseline for production shallow-water systems.

VORTICITY–STREAMFUNCTIONWhen two-dimensional incompressible rotation is the protagonist.

Transport vorticity and solve a Poisson problem for velocity, while remembering that topology contributes harmonic periods.

VARIATIONAL / GEOMETRICWhen discrete symmetry and long-time structure drive the design.

Derive the step from a discrete action or bracket so Kelvin circulation, momentum maps, or energy behavior shape the method from the start.
