Following Exterior Calculus in Graphics · begin with the dual pairing

An arrow
and a measurement.

A tangent vector v in the tangent space is an arrow: it can be a particle's instantaneous motion. A covector eta in the cotangent space is a linear measuring device: feed it an arrow and it returns one number.

a covector evaluates a tangent vector

For a fluid, the metric converts the velocity arrow u into the velocity one-form eta equals u flat. Locally it measures a tangent direction; along a whole curve it measures circulation.

the velocity one-form integrated along a curve

Only now do Clebsch variables enter: u flat equals d phi plus alpha d beta. They parameterize the one-form; they are not themselves the velocity, an evolution law, or an incompressibility condition.

Notes-guided path · Chapters 1, 2, and 5

From arrows to circulation.

The course notes build exterior calculus in this order: distinguish vectors from covectors; interpret forms by what they integrate over; introduce d, wedge, and Stokes; add the metric and Hodge star; then treat fluid velocity as a one-form. The Clebsch formula is our worked example in that language.

0
DUAL PAIRING

An arrow is not a covector.

A vector v is a possible tangent displacement or velocity. A covector eta at p is a linear function of such displacements. Picture the vector as an arrow and the covector as a stack of local contour lines: the pairing counts signed contour crossings.

NO METRIC REQUIRED
a covector maps an arrow to a scalar
This is a dual pairing, not a dot product. A metric is extra structure and is not needed merely to evaluate η on v.
1
MICROSCOPIC ↔ MACROSCOPIC

A one-form has two compatible pictures.

Microscopically, eta at p acting on v measures one tangent arrow. Macroscopically, the integral of eta along gamma adds those infinitesimal measurements along an oriented curve. For fluid velocity this integral is circulation.

FROM LOCAL READINGS TO A CURVE
integrate a one-form along a parameterized curve
DEC stores this macroscopic quantity on an edge: the edge number approximates ∫e η. It is not saying that the smooth one-form exists only on edges.

CHOOSE THREE 0-FORMS

Paint three scalar fields.

The fields phi, alpha, and beta map the surface to real numbers. They are not vectors or coordinates that the surface must obey. In this lab α and β are label fields; φ supplies an exact potential contribution.

the three controlled scalar fields

EXTERIOR DERIVATIVE

A scalar derivative is a one-form.

For a tangent displacement v, the number d alpha of v is the instantaneous change of α along v. This definition needs no metric. Only after applying sharp g may the lab draw a gradient arrow.

COORDINATE CALCULATION
differentiate the two labels
On the Euclidean plane, (dα)♯ = ∇α. On a curved surface, the metric changes the conversion from covector to vector.
4
WEDGE + STOKES

A two-form measures oriented patches.

The value d alpha wedge d beta evaluated on v and w is the signed determinant of the label changes along two tangent directions. For velocity, the vorticity two-form d u flat measures the circulation around the patch boundary.

THE LOCAL AND INTEGRAL PICTURES AGREE
Stokes and the planar Clebsch vorticity
At θ = 0 the two label differentials are parallel, so their determinant and the resolved circulation density both vanish.

CLEBSCH PARAMETERIZATION

Now assemble the velocity one-form.

The scalar α scales the one-form d beta pointwise. Adding d phi gives a velocity covector. The surface metric then supplies the velocity arrow that actually transports a particle.

WHY THE VORTICITY IS SIMPLE
assemble, raise the index, and differentiate
The identity d² = 0 removes the exact terms. On a closed loop, ∮dφ = 0 as well: the potential changes local velocity but not closed-loop circulation.

The whole story in one sentence

A velocity arrow moves a particle; its metric-dual one-form measures circulation; Clebsch variables parameterize that one-form; d turns it into vorticity; and a separate, metric-dependent Hodge projection enforces zero divergence.

This route follows Stephanie Wang, Mohammad Sina Nabizadeh, and Albert Chern, Exterior Calculus in Graphics: Course Notes for a SIGGRAPH 2023 Course, especially Chapters 1, 2, and 5. The notes provide the vector/covector and fluid-form language; this lab then applies that language to the Clebsch parameterization.

Smooth form first · discrete cochain second

What the mesh stores.

A smooth form is defined all over the surface by what it measures. DEC stores a finite set of those measurements as cochains: point samples on vertices, line integrals on oriented edges, and area integrals on oriented faces.

01 · 0-FORM SAMPLE

Evaluate α at mesh vertices

The smooth scalar exists between vertices too. Its discrete 0-cochain keeps only the sampled values α(vi).

02 · 1-FORM INTEGRAL

Integrate dα along each edge

On edge i → j, the integral of d alpha is alpha j minus alpha i. Reversing orientation reverses the sign.

03 · 2-FORM INTEGRAL

Integrate vorticity over a face

The face cochain stores the integral of d alpha wedge d beta over f: the signed circulation around that face.

04 · VELOCITY CIRCULATION

Measure u♭ along each edge

A discrete one-form stores line integrals, not vertex arrows. The metric and sharp g are needed to reconstruct a tangent velocity vector for drawing or advection.

05 · HODGE PROJECTION

Remove the exact 1-form dp

Solve Laplacian p = divergence u flat, then subtract d p. Curl is unchanged because d squared p is zero.

This smooth-to-discrete distinction follows the integration viewpoint in Exterior Calculus in Graphics and the primal-cochain implementation viewpoint in the Geometry Collective’s Discrete Exterior Calculus project.

GLOBAL CAVEAT

One Clebsch pair is not always globally complete.

Hodge decomposition with harmonic part h

Topology can support harmonic circulation that vorticity alone cannot recover. Clebsch potentials are also nonunique and may require multiple charts or multiple label pairs. The frog projection uses a finite Laplace–Beltrami basis, so it preserves resolved—not infinitely fine—vorticity.

What this buys a fluid solver

Why simulate with Clebsch variables?

The main appeal is structural: vorticity becomes the crossing of two advected scalar labels. But Clebsch variables are one possible representation of the velocity covector—not the only way to obtain circulation-preserving dynamics.

This route uses pullback/Lie transport of the velocity covector and does not require a Clebsch factorization. The exact pressure term changes neither vorticity nor closed-loop circulation.

STRUCTURE

Vorticity is built in.

The identity d u flat equals d alpha wedge d beta makes curl compatibility automatic and turns vortex transport into label transport.

VARIATIONAL FORM

Scalar fields expose constraints.

Clebsch potentials arise naturally as multipliers and canonical variables in variational or Hamiltonian descriptions. They separate vortical content from exact pressure-like motion.

PROJECTION

Exact corrections preserve vorticity.

A Hodge projection subtracts d p. Since d squared p is zero, the resolved vorticity survives the incompressibility solve.

What Clebsch variables do not solve

They do not enforce incompressibility by themselves. Advected labels can develop extremely fine gradients, so interpolation and numerical diffusion can still destroy their vorticity. Global topology may require harmonic modes or additional Clebsch pairs, and reconstructing a divergence-free velocity still requires a metric-dependent solve. For many solvers, transporting the velocity covector directly is simpler.
