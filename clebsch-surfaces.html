Begin with the object we actually want: a surface velocity

Clebsch,
one type at a time.

A fluid velocity u is a tangent vector field. Clebsch variables do not replace that physical velocity. They are three scalar functions used to parameterize its metric-dual one-form.

scalars build the velocity covector; the metric turns it into a vector

Two warnings up front: the formula is not a time-evolution law, and it does not automatically make the flow incompressible. It is a representation of velocity that makes vorticity especially transparent.

THE TARGET

Represent a tangent velocity.

At each point of the surface, the physical velocity u is a tangent vector. Differential-form notation first lowers its index: u flat is a covector. The covector measures a test displacement; the vector moves a particle.

VECTOR / COVECTOR PAIRING
u flat acting on v equals the metric inner product
In Cartesian coordinates the components happen to look identical. Their roles are still different.
1
CHOOSE THREE SCALARS

Paint three numbers on every point.

The fields phi, alpha, and beta map the surface to real numbers. They are not vectors. Here alpha and beta act as label fields; phi supplies an exact potential contribution.

THIS LAB'S PLANE FIELDS
the three controlled scalar fields
At θ = 0, β changes in the same direction as α. At θ = π/2, their changes are independent.
2
DIFFERENTIATE

A scalar derivative is a covector.

For a tangent displacement v, the number d alpha of v is the instantaneous change of α while moving along v. Only after applying the metric dual sharp g may we draw it as a gradient arrow.

COORDINATE CALCULATION
differentiate the two labels
On the Euclidean plane, (dα)♯ = ∇α. On a curved surface, the metric changes this conversion.
3
TAKE THE WEDGE

Crossing label changes produce oriented area.

The two-form d alpha wedge d beta measures the signed area spanned by the two covectors. In coordinates it is a determinant. If the rows are parallel, the determinant—and therefore the Clebsch vorticity—vanishes.

THE DETERMINANT
wedge product as a determinant
This is why the label-crossing slider is the cleanest experiment: θ = 0 forces zero vorticity.
4
ASSEMBLE VELOCITY

Scale one covector and add an exact one.

The product alpha d beta is simply the covector d beta multiplied pointwise by the scalar α. Add d phi, then use the metric to obtain the physical vector field.

WHY VORTICITY SIMPLIFIES
take one exterior derivative of velocity
The identity d² = 0 removes both potential terms. Thus φ can change velocity and divergence without changing vorticity.

The whole story in one sentence

Clebsch variables encode a velocity covector using three scalars; the crossing of two scalar differentials encodes its vorticity, the metric turns the covector into the velocity vector, and a separate Hodge projection is required if the flow must be divergence-free.

The same construction as discrete exterior calculus

Draw where every piece lives.

The smooth notation describes fields. On a triangle mesh, DEC stores their integrals as cochains. The exterior derivative becomes an incidence operation: subtract across an edge, then sum around a face.

01 · 0-FORMS

α, β, φ live at vertices

Each colored dot is one sampled number. A scalar is a 0-cochain on the mesh.

02 · 1-FORMS

d subtracts along oriented edges

The number on edge i → j is αj − αi. Reverse the edge and the sign reverses.

03 · 2-FORM

dα ∧ dβ lives on a face

The wedge measures oriented independence. Parallel label changes give zero; crossing changes give signed face circulation.

04 · VELOCITY 1-FORM

Assemble edge-wise readings

A discrete one-form records its integral along each oriented edge. An arrow appears only after the Hodge star/metric reconstructs a vector.

05 · HODGE PROJECTION

Remove the exact 1-form dp

Solve Laplacian p = divergence u flat, then subtract d p. Curl is unchanged because d squared p is zero.

Diagram convention follows the primal-cochain viewpoint in the Geometry Collective’s Discrete Exterior Calculus project: 0-cochains on vertices, 1-cochains on oriented edges, and 2-cochains on faces.

VECTORS

Metric-dependent tangent arrows that transport particles.

METRIC-FREE

Scalars, d, and ∧ are intrinsic.

metric-free part of the construction

These operations do not require embedding the surface in three-dimensional space. They make sense in any coordinate chart and agree where charts overlap.

METRIC-DEPENDENT

Arrows, divergence, and projection use g.

sharp and the codifferential depend on the metric

On a sphere, torus, or irregular frog mesh, the surface metric determines the arrow associated with a covector and the Hodge projection that removes divergence.

GLOBAL CAVEAT

One Clebsch pair is not always globally complete.

Hodge decomposition with harmonic part h

Topology can support harmonic circulation that vorticity alone cannot recover. Clebsch potentials are also nonunique and may require multiple charts or multiple label pairs. The frog projection uses a finite Laplace–Beltrami basis, so it preserves resolved—not infinitely fine—vorticity.
