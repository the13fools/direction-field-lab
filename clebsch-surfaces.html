The same construction as discrete exterior calculus

Draw where every piece lives.

The smooth notation describes fields. On a triangle mesh, DEC stores their integrals as cochains. The exterior derivative becomes an incidence operation: subtract across an edge, then sum around a face.

01 · 0-FORMS

α, β, φ live at vertices

Each colored dot is one sampled number. A scalar is a 0-cochain on the mesh.

02 · 1-FORMS

d subtracts along oriented edges

The number on edge i → j is αj − αi. Reverse the edge and the sign reverses.

03 · 2-FORM

dα ∧ dβ lives on a face

The wedge measures oriented independence. Parallel label changes give zero; crossing changes give signed face circulation.

04 · VELOCITY 1-FORM

Assemble edge-wise readings

A discrete one-form records its integral along each oriented edge. An arrow appears only after the Hodge star/metric reconstructs a vector.

05 · HODGE PROJECTION

Remove the exact 1-form dp

Solve Laplacian p = divergence u flat, then subtract d p. Curl is unchanged because d squared p is zero.

WHY CLEBSCH?

It separates how vorticity is encoded.

curl of Clebsch form

The potential φ changes velocity and divergence, but not vorticity. The two label fields encode vorticity through the crossing of their differentials.

WHY THE METRIC?

A covector becomes an arrow only after ♯.

flat and sharp musical maps

On the plane this conversion looks invisible in Cartesian coordinates. On a sphere, torus, or irregular frog mesh, the surface metric determines the arrow’s direction and length.

WHY PROJECT?

Clebsch form does not imply incompressibility.

Hodge projected velocity

Projection removes the gradient part responsible for sources and sinks. It preserves the resolved vorticity because the exterior derivative of an exact form is zero. The frog uses the finite loaded Laplace–Beltrami basis, so “resolved” is literal; harmonic circulation is not reconstructed from vorticity alone.
