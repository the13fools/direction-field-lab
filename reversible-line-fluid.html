The tempting idea, tested carefully

Project the line.
Then ask what moves.

An unoriented line has two local roots, u and −u. We can make both roots divergence-free with one symmetric Hodge projection. That is valid geometry. The harder question is whether both roots are simultaneous fluid velocities. This lab uses one exact, time-dependent Euler solution to locate the dividing line.

A projective Clebsch state on the cover

Clebsch still helps—

On the oriented double cover, a local Clebsch one-form can be odd under the sheet exchange. An odd pressure potential preserves that parity during the branchwise projection. This constructs a coherent projective momentum line. It does not by itself make the nonlinear Euler equation descend to an unoriented velocity downstairs.
