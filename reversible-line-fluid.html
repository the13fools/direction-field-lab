Why 4‑RoSy is not just “the line case twice”

Orthogonal roots exchange
curl and divergence.

On an oriented surface, let J rotate tangent vectors by 90°. A line contains u and −u; their divergence constraints are the same up to sign. A strict 4‑RoSy cross also contains Ju. Requiring that branch to be divergence-free imposes a second condition on the first branch.

quarter-turn exchanges the differential tests
exact cross plus branchwise div-free

Locally the first branch must be harmonic. On a flat torus, a smooth globally harmonic vector field is constant. That is why alternating “make it exactly 4‑RoSy” and “make every branch divergence-free” tends to erase the vortical content.

The last experiment is about dynamics, not topology alone

A 4‑RoSy cross is not
four simultaneous Euler fluids.

Opposite roots are candidates for time reversal only after time is reversed as well. The quarter-turn roots have no generic Euler relation to the first branch. If all four are evolved independently, the result is a multi-stream model with four velocities—not one ordinary fluid.

Where Clebsch variables still fit

Use them on a branch
or on the cover.

Once a branch is selected on a simply connected chart or covering sheet, it is an ordinary one-form and can use the ordinary Clebsch assembly. Chart changes may permute the branch or transform its potentials by gauge. Pressure projection acts on the assembled one-form, not on the scalar contour pictures separately.

one selected branch
branchwise projection

For a strict 4‑RoSy field, four independent pressure potentials generally break the quarter-turn relations. A coupled symmetric optimization must therefore choose a compromise or accept a much smaller harmonic solution space.
