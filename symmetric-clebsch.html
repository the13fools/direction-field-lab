This is the degree of freedom.

Clebsch variables are like a factorization with gauge freedom: several triples (φ, α, β) decode to the same one-form m. A canonical-looking choice can help with initialization or symmetry reduction, but the solver must preserve the decoded field—not a preferred contour picture.

One final sign that often looks like a bug

Mirror-symmetric vorticity
uses opposite colors.

The physical vorticity is a two-form, omega equals d m. Reflection reverses the oriented area element. Therefore its scalar color coefficient must change sign for the two-form itself to remain invariant.

orientation reversal
odd scalar coefficient

So teal above and orange below the centerline can certify symmetry. This is why the lab reports a vorticity-form defect, not a same-color defect.

Why a simulator should care

Symmetry is a debugging instrument.

MODELCheck the equations.

Symmetric geometry, boundary data, and initial physical velocity should produce a symmetric solution until an instability truly breaks it.

REPRESENTATIONDo not overconstrain the labels.

Demanding symmetric α, β, and φ separately can reject perfectly valid encodings of a symmetric flow.

DISCRETIZATIONFind numerical bias.

If m is symmetric analytically but the computed field is not, inspect the mesh, quadrature, boundary treatment, projection, and solver tolerance.
