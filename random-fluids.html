Guided derivation · about five minutes

From noisy scalars to an incompressible surface flow.

The central idea is simple but layered: randomness builds phi, alpha, beta; the Clebsch formula turns them into a velocity with vorticity; a Hodge solve keeps that vorticity while discarding the compressible part.

01TIME COHERENCE

Perlin noise moves coefficients—not geometry.

For each scalar f in {phi, alpha, beta}, the spatial functions are fixed first. The sphere uses real spherical harmonics. The tree frog uses eigenvectors of its cotangent Laplace–Beltrami operator. The periodic square uses Fourier eigenfunctions; the parameterized torus uses periodic coordinate modes. No ambient 3D plane wave is restricted onto either curved mesh.

f(x,t) = intrinsic spatial modes with smooth random coefficients
fixed intrinsic mode + smooth temporal Perlin coefficient
cotangent stiffness times mode = eigenvalue times lumped mass times mode
On the frog, K and M come from the actual triangles, so the modes depend on intrinsic mesh geometry—not its pose in space.

Each eigenfunction follows an independent 1D noise path N(o_j + r_j t). The turnover slider scales r_j; finer bands also evolve faster by roughly square root of band. The eigenvalue lambda j supplies the spatial frequency k j = square root of lambda j. At zero turnover, the random field freezes exactly.

02CLEBSCH ASSEMBLY

Two scalar gradients create the swirl.

Convert the three noisy scalars into the raw velocity one-form

u_C flat = d phi + alpha d beta
d phi is exact; alpha d beta couples two scalar fields.

Now take one exterior derivative:

d u_C flat = d alpha wedge d beta
because d squared phi = 0 and the product rule.

This is the useful Clebsch fact: vorticity is generated where the level sets of alpha and beta cross. The phi field can change the raw motion and its compression, but it contributes no vorticity.

ψSTREAM-FUNCTION INTUITION

Think of psi as a topographic map for the flow.

The gradient points uphill across the contour lines. Rotating that gradient by a quarter turn makes the velocity run along the contours instead. This is why a stream function produces circulating motion without sources or sinks.

FOLLOW CONTOURSvelocity follows psi contours
NO PILE-UPdivergence is zero
LOCAL SPINcurl equals Laplacian psi

The Poisson solve runs this backward: prescribe the desired local spin omega, then find the contour map psi whose Laplacian creates it. The absolute height of the map is irrelevant; only its slope moves the fluid.

03HODGE PROJECTION

Keep the resolved curl. Remove the collapse.

The raw Clebsch field is tangent, but it is not generally divergence-free. This implementation projects the velocity after it is assembled; it does not project phi, alpha, beta separately. On the frog, the same cotangent geometry underlies both the eigenmodes and the Poisson projection.

  1. measuremeasure Clebsch vorticity
  2. solvesolve Poisson equation
  3. rebuildrebuild projected velocity
KEPTresolved vorticity
REMOVEDgradient / compressible part
RESULTsurface divergence is approximately zero

The area-weighted mean vorticity is removed so the Poisson equation is solvable on a closed or periodic surface. On the torus, this stream-function reconstruction also omits any harmonic circulation component.

SURFACE
FIELD CONSTRUCTION
What exactly is being projected?

First construct the complete raw velocity raw Clebsch velocity. Its scalar vorticity is measured on the projection grid. A Poisson solve then reconstructs the coexact velocity carrying that resolved vorticity.

Why can raw Clebsch collapse?

Clebsch form controls vorticity, not divergence. The exact term d phi and part of alpha d beta can still create local sources and sinks. Particle density therefore changes even though the field is tangent to the surface.

Does projected mean curl-free?

No. The projected field is approximately divergence-free and deliberately vortical. Curl-free grad phi and divergence-free rotated grad psi are different pieces of the surface Hodge decomposition.

CHECK 01 · RAW

Vorticity does not imply incompressibility.

A raw Clebsch field can swirl beautifully while still focusing particles. Use divergence RMS to detect the compressible component that trails alone may disguise.

CHECK 02 · PROJECTED

The velocity changes; the target curl does not.

The solve does not merely “clean up” the arrows. It selects the coexact velocity associated with the resolved Clebsch vorticity, modulo discretization and omitted harmonic circulation.

CHECK 03 · PERLIN

Smooth randomness is not fluid dynamics.

Temporal Perlin noise prescribes how the random field changes; it is not a Navier–Stokes evolution. The particles respond to that kinematic velocity, but they do not feed back into it.
