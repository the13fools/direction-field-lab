Guided derivation · about five minutes

From noisy scalars to an incompressible surface flow.

The central idea is simple but layered: randomness builds φ, α, and β; the Clebsch formula turns them into a velocity with vorticity; a Hodge solve keeps that vorticity while discarding the compressible part.

01TIME COHERENCE

Perlin noise moves the modes—not the mesh.

For each scalar f ∈ {φ, α, β}, the seed first fixes spatial waves. On the square and torus these are periodic Fourier modes; on the sphere they are ambient plane waves restricted to the surface.

f(x,t) = Σ Am em(t)
· sin(qm(x) + θm + pm(t)) spatial wave + smooth temporal envelope + smooth temporal phase
1Hash a random slope at each integer time lattice point.
2Take each slope’s linear contribution inside the current interval.
3Blend them with 6ξ⁵ − 15ξ⁴ + 10ξ³ for smooth joins.

Each mode follows an independent 1D noise path N(om + rmt). The turnover slider scales rm; finer bands also evolve faster by roughly √band. At zero turnover, the random field freezes exactly.

02CLEBSCH ASSEMBLY

Two scalar gradients create the swirl.

Convert the three noisy scalars into the raw velocity one-form

uC♭ = dφ + α dβ dφ is exact; α dβ couples two scalar fields.

Now take one exterior derivative:

d uC♭ = dα ∧ dβ because d(dφ)=0 and d(αdβ)=dα∧dβ.

This is the useful Clebsch fact: vorticity is generated where the level sets of α and β cross. The φ field can change the raw motion and its compression, but it contributes no vorticity.

03HODGE PROJECTION

Keep the resolved curl. Remove the collapse.

The raw Clebsch field is tangent, but it is not generally divergence-free. This implementation projects the velocity after it is assembled; it does not project φ, α, or β separately.

  1. measureωC = ★ d uC♭
  2. solveΔS ψ = ωC
  3. rebuildu⊥ = J ∇S ψ
KEPTresolved vorticity
REMOVEDgradient / compressible part
RESULTdivS u⊥ ≈ 0

The area-weighted mean vorticity is removed so the Poisson equation is solvable on a closed or periodic surface. On the torus, this stream-function reconstruction also omits any harmonic circulation component.

    Hold the realization fixed.

  2. 02
    Compare raw with projected.

    Watch divergence RMS, not just the apparent swirl. Projection should suppress compression without erasing the resolved vorticity.

  3. 03
    Then animate the coefficients.

    Turnover moves amplitude and phase along smooth noise paths. Field correlation measures memory of the initial velocity.

SURFACE
FIELD CONSTRUCTION
What exactly is being projected?

First construct the complete raw velocity uC♭ = dφ + αdβ. Its scalar vorticity is measured on the projection grid. A Poisson solve then reconstructs the coexact velocity carrying that resolved vorticity.

Why can raw Clebsch collapse?

Clebsch form controls vorticity, not divergence. The exact term dφ and part of αdβ can still create local sources and sinks. Particle density therefore changes even though the field is tangent to the surface.

Does projected mean curl-free?

No. The projected field is approximately divergence-free and deliberately vortical. Curl-free grad φ and divergence-free J grad ψ are different pieces of the surface Hodge decomposition.

CHECK 01 · RAW

Vorticity does not imply incompressibility.

A raw Clebsch field can swirl beautifully while still focusing particles. Use divergence RMS to detect the compressible component that trails alone may disguise.

CHECK 02 · PROJECTED

The velocity changes; the target curl does not.

The solve does not merely “clean up” the arrows. It selects the coexact velocity associated with the resolved Clebsch vorticity, modulo discretization and omitted harmonic circulation.

CHECK 03 · PERLIN

Smooth randomness is not fluid dynamics.

Temporal Perlin noise prescribes how the random field changes; it is not a Navier–Stokes evolution. The particles respond to that kinematic velocity, but they do not feed back into it.
